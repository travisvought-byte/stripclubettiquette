# Looking out for each other

Peer safety for dancers: how to have each other's backs during a shift and on the way home, which customers to watch, and what to do when a coworker seems to be in trouble. A lot of this is what experienced dancers already do. It's written down here so newer dancers get it on day one.

## Get help now

| Who | How |
|---|---|
| **Emergency** | 911 |
| **National Human Trafficking Hotline** | Call 1-888-373-7888 (TTY 711), text 233733, or chat at [humantraffickinghotline.org/chat](https://humantraffickinghotline.org/chat). 24/7, confidential, not law enforcement. |
| **Sexual assault** | RAINN National Sexual Assault Hotline: call 800-656-4673 (800-656-HOPE), text HOPE to 64673, or chat at [hotline.rainn.org](https://hotline.rainn.org). 24/7, free, confidential, English and Spanish. |
| **Abusive partner** | National Domestic Violence Hotline: call 1-800-799-7233 (1-800-799-SAFE), or text START to 88788. 24/7. |
| **Crisis or overwhelmed** | 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline: call or text 988. 24/7, confidential. Spanish: call and press 2, or text AYUDA to 988. |

## Before the shift

- **Tell someone you trust** where you're working, when you expect to be done, and how you're getting home. Agree on a time to check in.
- **Share your location** with that person for the shift, and turn it off when you're home if you want.
- **Keep your own ID, phone, keys and money.** Nobody else should hold them, not a partner, a driver, a manager or a house mom.
- **Charge your phone** and carry a small charger.
- **Plan your ride home** before you start, so you're not deciding at 3 a.m. when you're tired.
- **Know the club.** Where are the panic buttons? Who's on security tonight? Which exits lead to the parking lot? A well-run club shows you on your first shift. See [what a club should provide](model-policies.md#what-every-dancer-can-expect).

## A buddy system at work

Pair up with at least one coworker each shift. It works best when it's simple and everyone knows the rules.

- **Check in after private dances and VIP time.** If your buddy goes into a private room, note the time. If they're not out when the time's up, look in or send security.
- **Agree on a signal.** A word or gesture that means "come get me" or "get security." Keep it the same every shift so it's automatic.
- **Watch each other's drinks.** If you drink at work, keep your glass with you or with your buddy. If someone suddenly seems much drunker than they should be, get them to security and away from customers.
- **Pass on warnings.** If a customer was rough, pushy or creepy with you, tell your buddy, the house mom and security before that customer moves on to someone else.
- **Leave together.** Walk out with your buddy or ask security to walk you to your car. In a club that follows good practice, security walks dancers out on request at any hour.
- **Check in after.** A quick "home safe" text closes the loop.

## Customers to watch

Most customers are fine. These are the ones to flag to security and your coworkers:

- They keep pushing after you say no: to touch, to go somewhere private, to stay longer.
- They try to get you alone, away from cameras or security.
- They ask your legal name, where you live, what car you drive, or when you get off.
- They offer big money to meet outside the club.
- They're waiting in the parking lot, or show up at more than one club where you work.
- They get angry, insulting or threatening when they don't get what he wants.
- They're far too drunk to control themselves.
- They take photos or video, or try to.

You can end any dance or conversation, for any reason. In a well-run club, security backs you up and the customer is removed. If your club makes you choose between your safety and your income, that's a reason to look it up in the [directory](../data/clubs.csv) and consider moving on.

## Getting home safely

- Walk out with a buddy or security. Have your keys in hand before you reach the door.
- Look around the lot and inside your car before you get in.
- **If you think you're being followed**, don't drive home. Drive somewhere busy and well lit, like a police station, fire station or open gas station, and call 911.
- Use a rideshare you booked yourself, and check the plate and the driver's name before you get in.
- Vary your route and your schedule if a customer has shown too much interest.

## Protect your privacy

- **Keep your stage name and your legal name apart.** Don't tell customers your legal name, your other job, your school or your neighborhood.
- **Separate your accounts.** Use a different phone number and social media accounts for work, and keep personal accounts private.
- **Check photos before you post.** Turn off location tags, and look for street signs, house numbers, your car's plate or your kids' school in the background.
- **If someone is stalking you**, the Stalking Prevention, Awareness and Resource Center recommends trusting your instincts, calling police if you're in immediate danger, keeping a log of every contact, saving every message, photo and post, and connecting with a local victim service provider to plan for your safety. Domestic violence and sexual assault programs often help with stalking too.

## When a coworker seems controlled or in danger

Sometimes the danger isn't a customer. It's the person a coworker came with. These signs together are worth taking seriously:

- Someone else holds their ID, phone or money, or takes their earnings at the end of the night.
- The same person drops them off and picks them up, and seems to be in charge.
- They have a quota to hit, or a debt they can never pay off.
- They seem afraid, watched, or not allowed to talk freely.
- They seem younger than 18.

The full list is in [Stop trafficking](human-trafficking.md#signs-to-watch-for).

**What you can do**

1. **If anyone is in immediate danger, call 911.**
2. **Talk privately, if it's safe.** In the dressing room, away from the person who seems to be in control. Keep it simple: "Are you OK? Is anyone making you do this?"
3. **Don't confront the person in control** and don't try to rescue anyone yourself. It can put your coworker, and you, in more danger.
4. **Give them a way to reach help.** The national hotline number, 1-888-373-7888 or text 233733, is short enough to memorize. Tell them it's free, confidential, open 24/7, and not the police.
5. **Let them decide.** Many people in controlling situations aren't ready to leave the first time someone asks. Being the person who noticed and didn't judge matters.
6. **Tell someone at the club you trust**, like the house mom, security or the manager who handles these concerns, if your club has one.
7. **Call the hotline yourself** for advice. You don't have to give your name. See [Reporting safely](human-trafficking.md#reporting-safely).
8. **If they may be under 18**, call the hotline or 911 right away. Anyone under 18 in commercial sex is a trafficking victim under federal law, whether or not anyone is using force.

## After an assault

- **Get safe first.** Call 911 if you're hurt or in danger.
- **Call RAINN** at 800-656-4673 for confidential support and to find a local sexual assault service provider, who can go with you to a hospital or help you decide what to do next.
- **You don't have to decide about police right away.** But if you might want to report later, it helps to save texts, write down what happened while you remember it, and ask the club to save camera footage.
- **Tell your manager** so the customer is banned and other dancers are protected. In Washington, a club must ban a customer for at least three years when an accusation of violence is supported by evidence.

## Take care of yourself

Late nights, cash, alcohol and difficult customers wear anyone down. If you're feeling hopeless, overwhelmed or unsafe with yourself, call or text 988. For low-cost medical, dental and mental health care, find a community health center through the federal [Find a Health Center](https://findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov/) tool. Health centers serve people whether or not they have insurance and charge on a sliding scale based on what you can pay.

More on your rights, pay and getting help: [For dancers](for-dancers.md).
