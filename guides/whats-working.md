# What's working

Fee rules, safety steps and business models that clubs and lawmakers have tried, with what the record shows about each. Every point links to its source. A lawsuit is an allegation, a settlement is not an admission, and a court ruling is a finding.

## Washington's 2024 law

Washington's ESSB 6105 (Chapter 250, Laws of 2024) is the most detailed dancer-protection law in the country. It passed the Senate 29 to 20 and the House 58 to 36. ([Final bill report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Biennium/2023-24/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/Senate/6105-S.E%20SBR%20FBR%2024.pdf))

**Fees, in effect January 1, 2025** ([final bill report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Biennium/2023-24/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/Senate/6105-S.E%20SBR%20FBR%2024.pdf))

- A house or "leasing" fee has to be the same for every dancer in the club, in a written contract, and fixed for at least three months.
- No late fees, no interest, no fee for missing a shift, and no carrying unpaid fees forward. That ends "back rent."
- A fee can't be more than the dancer took in during that period. Over any 8 hours it's capped at the lesser of $150 or 30% of what the dancer collected outside private rooms, plus 30% of what the dancer collected in private rooms.
- The contract has to explain how the club estimates a dancer's take over 8 hours.
- The state's rules add three more points: a room fee charged when no dance happens doesn't count as money the dancer collected, clubs must post signs saying dancers can't be made to hand over tips, and interfering with a dancer's music can count as retaliation. ([L&I rulemaking order, December 2024](https://lni.wa.gov/rulemaking-activity/AO24-09/2409CR103.pdf))

**Safety**

- Panic buttons in every room where a dancer may be alone with a customer, with proof sent to the state each year. ([Final bill report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Biennium/2023-24/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/Senate/6105-S.E%20SBR%20FBR%2024.pdf))
- At least one person on site whose main job is security, keypad locks on dressing rooms, cleaning supplies, and posted customer-etiquette signs. ([Final bill report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Biennium/2023-24/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/Senate/6105-S.E%20SBR%20FBR%2024.pdf))
- Outside training for all non-dancer staff on sexual harassment, workplace assault, human trafficking, de-escalation and first aid, repeated every two years. Dancers may take it too. ([Final bill report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Biennium/2023-24/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/Senate/6105-S.E%20SBR%20FBR%2024.pdf))
- Written policies on customer complaints, violence and ejections, and written reasons for any firing or refusal to rehire within 10 business days. ([Final bill report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Biennium/2023-24/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/Senate/6105-S.E%20SBR%20FBR%2024.pdf); [bill text, section 2(6)](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2023-24/Pdf/Bills/Senate%20Bills/6105-S.E.pdf))

**Alcohol**

- The state liquor board had to repeal the rule that kept alcohol out of strip clubs, and the labor department has to inspect a club within 90 days of its liquor license application. ([Final bill report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Biennium/2023-24/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/Senate/6105-S.E%20SBR%20FBR%2024.pdf))
- In June 2024 a Seattle club became the first in Washington in nearly 50 years to get a liquor license. ([The Columbian, from The Seattle Times](https://columbian.com/news/2024/jun/17/seattle-strip-club-becomes-first-in-washington-to-serve-alcohol-in-decades))

**Why dancers pushed for it**

- A 2020 state advisory committee report put house fees at $65 to $165 a shift, with the average above $100. ([Cascade PBS](https://www.cascadepbs.org/politics/2023/02/seattle-exotic-dancers-organize-improve-predatory-conditions))
- With no alcohol to sell, clubs lived off dancer fees, dancers told KUOW. They said Portland's clubs, which serve alcohol, recovered from the pandemic and Seattle's didn't. ([KUOW](https://www.kuow.org/politics/2024-02-19/strippers-washington-state-seattle-worst-places-to-work-bill-of-rights))
- In February 2024 Washington had 12 strip clubs left, and none served alcohol. ([Spokesman-Review](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2024/feb/08/alcohol-in-strip-clubs-washington-senate-approves-))
- The club owners' lobbyist said owners doubted the training and security rules were realistic, but broadly supported the bill's goals. ([KUOW](https://www.kuow.org/politics/2024-02-19/strippers-washington-state-seattle-worst-places-to-work-bill-of-rights))

**Not known yet:** whether fees fell, dancer earnings rose or clubs did better after 2025. If you dance or run a club in Washington, [report your club's fees anonymously](../data/dancer-reports.csv).

## Business models that treat dancers better

**Employee status, with a choice.** Results are mixed, and the details matter.

- Spearmint Rhino settled a class action for nearly $13 million and agreed to stop treating dancers as independent contractors and to stop charging stage fees. ([NBC Los Angeles, 2012](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/Strippers-Win-13-Million-Settlement-Wages-Dispute-179336841.html))
- Deja Vu-affiliated clubs in 18 states settled for $6.5 million. Dancers could choose employee status, and those who stayed contractors shared a $4.5 million pool of credits against club fees. ([CBS News and AP, 2017](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/judge-orders-millions-in-back-wages-to-28000-exotic-dancers))
- RCI Hospitality says its entertainers are independent contractors, but dancers at two of its clubs, in Minneapolis and Phoenix, can choose to be commissioned employees. ([RCI annual report, fiscal 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828026019804/rick-20250930.htm))
- A Washington club owner told Cascade PBS that dancers at that owner's clubs can choose, and nearly all pick contractor status. ([Cascade PBS](https://www.cascadepbs.org/politics/2023/02/seattle-exotic-dancers-organize-improve-predatory-conditions))
- After California's AB 5, some clubs cut hours, took more of the tips or added fees. Dancers reported one club taking an $11 "shoe allowance" out of dance earnings. ([San Francisco Examiner](https://www.sfexaminer.com/news/the-lusty-lady-led-the-charge-will-todays-sex-workers-follow/article_c6b28854-50a1-11ed-a50b-6729f88abb39.html); [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/11737567/strippers-clash-over-employment-status-in-dueling-l-a-protests))
- At Star Garden, employee dancers had to sell $200 a shift in private dances before they got a cut. ([In These Times](https://inthesetimes.com/article/off-the-pole-onto-the-picket-line))
- Not every settlement changes practice. Scarlett's Cabaret's $6 million settlement, covering clubs in Florida and Ohio, didn't require the owners to change their labor practices. ([Insurance Journal and AP, 2015](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2015/07/07/374211.htm)) Spearmint Rhino's settlement did: dancers were to be reclassified as employees within six months, and house fees ended. ([Capital & Main, 2012](https://capitalandmain.com/judge-strippers-are-not-independent-contractors))

The lesson from these cases: employee status helps when it doesn't come with new quotas and fees. A club that offers it should publish the whole package: wage, commission, quota and any deductions.

**A union.** Star Garden in North Hollywood became the first unionized strip club in the U.S., with Actors' Equity, in 2023.

- Dancers organized because guards didn't step in when customers threatened them, and because they were filmed without consent. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1134667170))
- In the settlement, the owners recognized the union, agreed to start bargaining within 30 days, and agreed to reopen and bring back dancers who'd been let go. None of the former guards could be assigned to the club. ([Actors' Equity](https://actorsequity.org/news/PR/2023/05/16/StarGardenVictory))
- We haven't confirmed whether a first contract was signed.

**A worker-owned club.** San Francisco's Lusty Lady unionized in 1997 and ran as a worker-owned co-op for about ten years from 2003. Dancers were paid hourly and kept all their tips. It closed in 2013, reportedly because it couldn't pay the rent. ([San Francisco Examiner](https://www.sfexaminer.com/news/the-lusty-lady-led-the-charge-will-todays-sex-workers-follow/article_c6b28854-50a1-11ed-a50b-6729f88abb39.html))

## Safety steps any club can copy

- Washington's 2019 law required panic buttons, and required clubs to keep a list of customers accused of violence and turn them away. ([ESSB 6105 final bill report, background](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Biennium/2023-24/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/Senate/6105-S.E%20SBR%20FBR%2024.pdf))
- The 2019 bill as reported had clubs keep accusations against a customer for 5 years and bar the customer for at least 3 years when the accusation was sworn or backed by other evidence. Clubs under the same owner had to share the list. ([House bill report, HB 1756](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Biennium/2019-20/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/House/1756%20HBR%20LAWS%2019.pdf))
- Before the 2024 law, dancers said some struggling clubs had no security at all, managers pushed them to dance for customers known to be violent, and some dancers paid security to walk them to their cars. ([KUOW](https://www.kuow.org/politics/2024-02-19/strippers-washington-state-seattle-worst-places-to-work-bill-of-rights))
- After repeated gun violence, a 2024 nuisance settlement permanently closed Jaguars Gold Club in El Paso. Any new business on the site must use off-duty police officers, metal-detector wands and bag checks, brighter lighting, 30-day video retention and new managers. Those terms show what authorities expect once things go wrong. ([El Paso Times, via Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/jaguars-strip-club-permanently-closed-124453656.html))
- RCI says it requires its Texas alcohol servers to be state-certified and has a "no tolerance" drug policy. ([RCI annual report, fiscal 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828026019804/rick-20250930.htm))

## Clear, honest pricing

- A federal court found that Rick's Cabaret New York kept $2 of every $24 "Dance Dollar" bought by credit card without telling customers. The court also found its fines, fees and required tips to staff broke New York labor law. ([Court opinion, S.D.N.Y. No. 09-cv-3043](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1:2009cv03043/343172/628/0.pdf))
- Before the 2024 law, Washington dancers reported fees that went up for dancers who worked fewer shifts, a $20 charge each time a dancer stepped outside, and dancers paying for customers' card chargebacks. ([Cascade PBS](https://www.cascadepbs.org/politics/2023/02/seattle-exotic-dancers-organize-improve-predatory-conditions))
- The model to copy is Washington's rule: one written fee schedule, the same for every dancer, fixed for at least three months, with no fines or carried-over debt. ([Final bill report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Biennium/2023-24/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/Senate/6105-S.E%20SBR%20FBR%2024.pdf)) We ask every club to [publish its fees](../data/club-info.csv) the same way.

## Where the money comes from at the largest public operator

RCI Hospitality runs about 70 locations in 15 states, including Rick's Cabaret and Tootsie's. These figures are from its annual reports.

- **Fiscal 2025 sales:** 40% service (cover charges, licensing fees and room rentals), 43% alcohol, and 17% food, merchandise and other. ([Annual report, fiscal 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828026019804/rick-20250930.htm))
- **Fiscal 2022 sales:** 45% service, 39% alcohol, and 16% food and other. ([Annual report, fiscal 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828022031907/rick-20220930.htm))
- At the biggest operator, then, drinks and food bring in more than half the money. Clubs that couldn't sell drinks, as in Washington before 2024, leaned on dancer fees instead. ([KUOW](https://www.kuow.org/politics/2024-02-19/strippers-washington-state-seattle-worst-places-to-work-bill-of-rights))
- RCI says clubs compete on "brand name recognition" and a "distinctive entertainment environment." ([Annual report, fiscal 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828026019804/rick-20250930.htm))
- It prices club purchases on past earnings, looks for a 25% to 33% cash-on-cash return, sells clubs that underperform, and owns much of its real estate (66 properties in 2022). ([Annual report, fiscal 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828022031907/rick-20220930.htm))
- The same report cites an industry group's estimate of more than 2,200 adult nightclubs in North America, with more than $5 billion in yearly revenue. ([Annual report, fiscal 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828022031907/rick-20220930.htm))

## When business slows, and why clubs close

- RCI calls the industry "extremely sensitive to the general local economy," and its revenue usually dips from April through September. ([Annual report, fiscal 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828026019804/rick-20250930.htm))
- In fiscal 2022 its same-store club sales rose 10.1%, which it linked to stimulus money. ([Annual report, fiscal 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828022031907/rick-20220930.htm))
- In fiscal 2025 its same-store club sales fell 2.1%. It closed three Bombshells sports bars and a food hall, wrote down the licenses of six clubs by $3.8 million, and shifted to a plan focused on improving the clubs it already has. ([Annual report, fiscal 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828026019804/rick-20250930.htm))
- In September 2025 RCI and several people were indicted in New York on bribery and tax-fraud charges tied to sales tax on "Dance Dollars." Charged is not convicted, and the company says it will fight the charges. ([Annual report, fiscal 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828026019804/rick-20250930.htm))

**Closures, and the reasons given**

- **Deja Vu Showgirls, Spokane Valley, Washington (closed summer 2023):** a dancer said fees went up in its last months, many weekends drew a dozen or fewer customers, and alcohol sales might have kept it open. That's one dancer's view, not a finding. ([Spokesman-Review](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2024/feb/08/alcohol-in-strip-clubs-washington-senate-approves-))
- **Centerfolds, Las Vegas (closed April 2025):** an employee said the club was paying about $15,000 a month for a burlesque show that wasn't bringing in business. The venue reopened under new management with a sports theme. ([Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/vitalvegas/longtime-vegas-strip-club-centerfolds-to-close/); [Las Vegas Review-Journal](https://neon.reviewjournal.com/?p=3310347))
- **Pharaoh's, Cheektowaga, New York (closed September 2025):** the owner was awaiting sentencing after a conviction, and the liquor license had lapsed. Another owner said it would be leased to new management. ([Ground News](https://ground.news/article/pharaohs-strip-club-to-be-leased-to-new-management-with-new-name))
- **Jaguars Gold Club, El Paso (closed 2024):** under the nuisance settlement above. ([El Paso Times, via Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/jaguars-strip-club-permanently-closed-124453656.html))
- **Lusty Lady, San Francisco (closed 2013):** reportedly over rent. ([San Francisco Examiner](https://www.sfexaminer.com/news/the-lusty-lady-led-the-charge-will-todays-sex-workers-follow/article_c6b28854-50a1-11ed-a50b-6729f88abb39.html))
- **Washington clubs generally:** a dancer group cited too few customers per dancer to cover fees, and blamed restrictive rules and managers who did little to promote their clubs. ([Strippers Are Workers and Working Washington](https://workingwa.org/strippersareworkers/housefees))

## What the record points to

1. **Sell drinks and food instead of squeezing dancers.** Washington dancers made that case ([KUOW](https://www.kuow.org/politics/2024-02-19/strippers-washington-state-seattle-worst-places-to-work-bill-of-rights)), and RCI's sales mix fits it ([annual report, fiscal 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/935419/000162828026019804/rick-20250930.htm)).
2. **Raising fees as business falls can drive dancers away.** That's the account from Spokane Valley, one example. ([Spokesman-Review](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2024/feb/08/alcohol-in-strip-clubs-washington-senate-approves-))
3. **Security and clean management protect the license.** The clubs in El Paso and Cheektowaga were lost over violence and a conviction. ([El Paso Times, via Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/jaguars-strip-club-permanently-closed-124453656.html); [Ground News](https://ground.news/article/pharaohs-strip-club-to-be-leased-to-new-management-with-new-name))
4. **Local fees matter too.** San Diego dancers and owners together lobbied against city permit fees rising to $388 per dancer and $5,830 per club each year. ([KRON and AP, 2018](https://digital-release.kron4.com/news/san-diego-strippers-club-owners-protest-fees-as-too-high/amp))
