# Laws by state

Plain-English summary of the laws that shape how clubs operate. This is not legal advice. Laws change, so check the linked source before relying on anything here.

Last verified: November 22, 2026

## Federal baseline

- **Sex trafficking.** Using force, fraud or coercion to get an adult to perform a commercial sex act, or involving anyone under 18 in commercial sex, is a federal crime (18 U.S.C. 1591). For minors, prosecutors don't have to show force, fraud or coercion. A commercial sex act is any sex act where anything of value is given or received. Sources: [DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/humantrafficking), [18 U.S.C. 1591](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t18/s1591/)
- **Wages.** Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, courts look at the economic reality of the job, not the contract's label. Many courts have found dancers to be employees, including the Third Circuit in 2019. Source: [Verma v. 3001 Castor](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca3/18-2462/18-2462-2019-08-30.html)

## Ohio

- **Hours:** sexually oriented businesses can't be open from midnight to 6 a.m. A club with a liquor permit may stay open until its permit hour, but only if it doesn't offer nude entertainment.
- **No touching:** customers may not knowingly touch a nude or semi-nude employee or the employee's clothing, and nude or semi-nude employees may not touch customers or other employees, unless they're immediate family. It's a first-degree misdemeanor if a specified anatomical area (or the clothing over it) is touched, and a fourth-degree misdemeanor otherwise.
- **Trafficking poster:** the state makes a hotline poster available and encourages, but doesn't require, adult entertainment establishments to display it.
- **State hotline:** 844-END-OHHT (844-363-6448), run 24/7 by the Ohio Bureau of Criminal Investigation.
- **Local rules:** cities and townships zone and license adult businesses. Example: [Cincinnati Code Chapter 899](https://www.cincinnati-oh.gov/finance/linkservid/8D35B185-003D-BB43-494824B44B44EAA1/showMeta/0/).

Sources: [ORC 2907.40](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2907.40), [ORC 5502.63](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5502.63), [Ohio Attorney General](https://ohioattorneygeneral.gov/ENDOHHT)

## Washington (ESSB 6105, 2024)

The most detailed dancer protections in the country.

- No one under 18 on the premises. Where alcohol is served, no one under 21.
- Non-entertainer staff trained on preventing harassment and assault, identifying and reporting trafficking, de-escalation and first aid, then retrained at least every two years. Entertainers can opt in.
- Panic buttons in every room where an entertainer may be alone with a customer, and in restrooms and dressing rooms, with proof of compliance filed with the state at least once a year.
- At least one dedicated security worker on site during all open hours.
- Keypad entry for dressing rooms, cleaning supplies at stages, written procedures for violent or rule-breaking customers, and signs at entrances pointing customers to etiquette resources.
- Allegations of trafficking, prostitution or violence against an entertainer must be recorded. When an allegation is backed by a sworn statement or other evidence, the customer is banned for at least 3 years, including at commonly owned clubs.
- **Fees:** the same written fees for everyone, in effect for at least 3 months. In any 8-hour period, fees can't exceed the lesser of $150 or 30% of the entertainer's earnings outside private areas, plus 30% of private-area earnings. No late fees, missed-shift fees, interest or carried-forward balances, and fees can't exceed what the entertainer earned. The $150 cap adjusts for inflation every two years starting in 2027.
- Signs in entertainer areas saying tips don't have to be handed over. Written reasons for termination within 10 business days.
- Clubs can now get liquor licenses.
- Effective June 6, 2024, except the safety and fee sections, which took effect January 1, 2025.

Source: [ESSB 6105 session law](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2023-24/Htm/Bills/Session%20Laws/Senate/6105-S.SL.htm)

## Texas

- Minimum age of 21 to work at a sexually oriented business in any job, from dancer to janitor (SB 315, 2021). A federal district court upheld it in 2024, and the Fifth Circuit upheld it in 2025.

Sources: [Fifth Circuit opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/24/24-50434-CV0.pdf), [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/texas-law-banning-strip-club-workers-under-21-survives-challenge), [Law360](https://law360.com/employment-authority/labor/articles/2364476/5th-circ-upholds-law-banning-strip-club-workers-under-age-21)

## Florida

- Adult entertainment establishments can't employ or contract with anyone under 21 (HB 7063, effective July 1, 2024). A federal challenge was dropped in May 2025, after the Eleventh Circuit upheld a similar Jacksonville ordinance.

Sources: [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/florida-age-restriction-adult-entertainment-free-speech-lawsuit-rcna160328), [WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/courts-law/2025-05-29/challenge-to-floridas-stripper-age-law-dropped-judge-closes-case)

## Louisiana

- Entertainers whose breasts or buttocks are exposed at alcohol-licensed venues must be at least 21 (2016 law). The Fifth Circuit upheld it in November 2018.

Sources: [Doe v. Landry, 5th Cir. 2018](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca5/17-30292/17-30292-2018-11-16.html), [AP via Arkansas Democrat-Gazette](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2018/nov/17/louisiana-age-limit-dancers-upheld/)

## California

- Under the state's ABC test for employment (Dynamex, 2018, and AB 5, 2019), dancers are generally employees. AB 5 did not exempt exotic dancers. Many clubs reclassified dancers, and dancers are split on whether it helped.

Sources: [ACLU of Southern California](https://www.aclusocal.org/en/node/3751), [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2020/01/24/california-labor-law-independent-contractors-strippers/)

## The age-21 debate

Lawmakers in Texas, Florida and Louisiana said raising the minimum age protects young women from trafficking and exploitation. Dancers and clubs who sued argued these laws restrict lawful work and expression, and Florida's challengers argued the state hadn't shown a link between adult clubs and trafficking. Federal appeals courts have so far upheld the laws. Sources: [CBS Miami](https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/florida-stripper-law-challenge-put-on-hold), [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/sex-shops-ask-fifth-circuit-to-strip-texas-age-limit-on-workers/)
