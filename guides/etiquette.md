# Strip club etiquette

How to be a good customer: respectful, safe, and not broke at the end of the night.

## The short version

1. Bring cash in small bills and set a budget before you walk in.
2. If you sit at the stage, tip every dancer who performs while you're there.
3. Ask the price before any private dance or VIP time starts. Every time.
4. Follow the club's rules and the dancer's lead. "No" means no and "stop" means stop.
5. Don't touch unless the club's rules and the law allow it. In Ohio, touching a nude or semi-nude dancer is a crime.
6. No photos or video. Ever.
7. Don't ask for personal information (real name, number, social media, where they live) or to meet outside the club.
8. Never ask for "extras." It's illegal and it puts the dancer at risk.
9. Tip the bartenders and servers, and treat security and staff with respect.
10. Plan your ride home before your first drink.

## Before you go

- **Age:** expect 18+ at clubs without alcohol and 21+ where alcohol is served. Some clubs set 21+ regardless. Bring valid ID.
- **Check the club:** hours, cover charge, dress code, and whether it serves alcohol or is BYOB. The club's website or social page usually says.
- **Cash:** ATMs inside clubs often charge high fees. Many clubs also sell "dance dollars" (club currency you buy with a card) with a surcharge. Ask what the surcharge is before you buy.
- **Budget:** decide what you'll spend before you arrive. Leave the rest at home.

## Know the local rules

Rules vary by state and city. In Ohio, customers can't knowingly touch a nude or semi-nude dancer or the dancer's clothing, and dancers can't touch customers. It's a misdemeanor for both, and clubs can lose their liquor permits over it. Other places range from strict no-contact rules to limited contact. When in doubt, keep your hands to yourself and follow the posted rules and the dancer's direction. See [laws-by-state.md](laws-by-state.md).

## At the door

Have your ID out, pay the cover, and don't argue with security. They're there to protect the dancers and you.

## At the stage

- Seats along the stage (the "rail") come with an expectation: tip each dancer who performs while you're sitting there.
- Hand tips to the dancer or put them where the dancer indicates. Don't throw coins or crumpled bills.
- Don't wave money for attention and then not tip.
- Not planning to tip? Sit back from the stage.

## Private dances

- Confirm the price per song and how many songs before it starts.
- Pay what you agreed, for the songs you got.
- The dancer sets the boundaries, along with the law and the club's rules. If the dancer moves your hands or says stop, stop.
- Don't haggle mid-dance or try to renegotiate afterward.

## VIP and champagne rooms

This is where most disputes happen. Before you go in, ask:

- How long, and what's the total price?
- What's included (drinks, how many dancers)?
- Who do you pay: the club, the dancer, or both? Is a tip expected on top?

Get an itemized receipt, keep your card in your own hand, and never sign a slip that isn't itemized. If something's off, talk to a manager before you leave, and check your card statement the next day.

## Talking with dancers

- They're working. Be friendly, but don't take up a dancer's time for long without buying a dance or a drink, if that's the custom.
- Don't ask for real names, phone numbers, social media, or where they live. Don't follow anyone or wait outside after a shift.
- Don't comment on bodies or lecture anyone about their life choices.
- If a dancer says no thanks, accept it and move on.

## Phones, photos and privacy

Never photograph or record dancers or other customers. Many dancers keep their work private for their own safety, and a photo can expose them to stalking or harassment. Most clubs will throw you out for it.

## Alcohol and behavior

Pace yourself. Belligerent or sloppy customers get removed. Never drive impaired.

## Protect your money

- Keep your card in your control and your tab itemized.
- Watch your drink.
- Count your cash before you go in so you know where you stand.

## Groups and bachelor parties

- Pick one person to handle money and confirm prices for the group.
- No pranks involving a dancer without the dancer's agreement.
- Look after the guest of honor. Don't let them overspend or overdrink.

## Women and couples

Most clubs welcome women and couples. Same rules.

## If something seems wrong

If a dancer seems afraid, controlled by someone else, or underage, don't confront anyone or try a rescue. Note what you saw, then call or text the **National Human Trafficking Hotline: 1-888-373-7888, text 233733**. In Ohio you can also report to **844-END-OHHT**. Call 911 if someone is in immediate danger. See [human-trafficking.md](human-trafficking.md) for what to look for.

## Why this matters

Washington State now requires clubs to post signs at the entrance pointing customers to etiquette resources. Good customers make clubs safer, and safer clubs are better for everyone in the building.
