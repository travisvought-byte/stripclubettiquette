# Model policies and procedures for clubs

A template any club can adopt to protect dancers, staff and customers. Copy it, replace the parts in [brackets], and cut or tighten anything that doesn't fit your state and city. This is a template, not legal advice. Have a lawyer who knows your state's labor, licensing and adult-business rules review your final version before you adopt it.

It's built from Washington State's 2024 dancer-protection law (the most detailed in the country), federal wage, tip and workplace-safety guidance, federal anti-trafficking guidance, and the [Club Safety Standard](../standards/club-safety-standard.md). Where a point comes from Washington's law, that's a benchmark: it's the law there and a good practice everywhere else.

## How to use this template

1. Put your club's name, address and contacts in the brackets.
2. Name a person for every role in [Roles and responsibilities](#roles-and-responsibilities). One person can hold more than one role in a small club, but every duty needs a name.
3. Give every staff member and every dancer a copy before their first shift, and keep a signed receipt.
4. Post the short versions (the hotline poster, the fee schedule, the customer rules) where the policy says to.
5. Review it once a year and after any serious incident.

> **Adopted by:** [Club name], [address]. **Effective:** [date]. **Policy owner:** [name, title]. **Next review:** [date].

## Our commitments

[Club name] commits to the following.

- Every dancer is safe at work, can end any dance or conversation at any time, and never has to choose between safety and income.
- Every dancer is paid directly, keeps their own tips, and never owes the club money.
- No one under the legal age works or is admitted. No one is controlled, coerced or sold here.
- Anyone can raise a concern without being punished for it.
- We follow federal, state and local law. Where this policy is stricter than the law, we follow this policy.

## Roles and responsibilities

Every role below has duties toward dancer safety. Write the name of the person in each role on the copy you post.

| Role | Duties toward dancer safety |
|---|---|
| **Owner** | Pays for security, panic buttons, cameras, lighting and training. Adopts this policy and reviews it every year. Makes sure no one, including the owner, takes dancers' tips or collects a dancer's pay for someone else. |
| **General manager** | Runs the policy day to day. Approves every ban. Keeps the incident log, ban list, training records and fee schedule. Makes sure every shift has a manager on duty and dedicated security. |
| **Manager on duty** | Opens and closes the shift checklist. Responds to every incident and fills in the report before the shift ends. Decides on ejections with security. Calls 911 when anyone is in danger. |
| **Trafficking-concern lead** | One named manager who takes every trafficking or coercion concern, decides next steps, contacts the hotline or police, and keeps those reports private. Has a named backup for days off. |
| **Security** | Watches interactions between dancers and customers, especially near private rooms. Responds to panic buttons right away. Gives warnings, removes customers, and walks dancers to their cars on request. During busy hours, does security and nothing else. |
| **Door staff** | Checks every customer's ID for age. Refuses entry to anyone on the ban list. Watches the parking lot and entrance for anyone waiting on a dancer. |
| **House mom or dressing-room attendant** | Keeps the dressing room locked and private. Checks in on dancers during the shift. Is a safe person to tell about a problem. Never collects fees, holds IDs or keeps a dancer's money or phone. |
| **DJ** | Runs the stage rotation fairly. Can see the floor and alerts security to trouble. Announces house rules for customers. Never uses music, stage time or rotation to punish a dancer who complains. |
| **Bartenders and servers** | Serve alcohol responsibly and cut off customers who are too drunk. Never pressure dancers to drink. Report customers who harass dancers or ask about "extras." |
| **Floor and VIP hosts** | Quote prices and times to customers up front. Keep private-room times accurate. Never steer customers to a dancer for sex or "extras." Check on dancers when a private session ends. |

### What every dancer can expect

- A copy of this policy, the fee schedule and the customer rules before the first shift.
- The location of every panic button, and how they work.
- A safe way to report a problem to someone other than the person it's about.
- Their own ID, phone and money, always.

## Policies and procedures

### 1. Security and escorts

**Policy.** At least one person whose main job is security is on site during all open hours, watching interactions between dancers and customers. During peak hours, security does nothing else. (Washington requires this.)

**Procedure.**

1. The manager on duty confirms security is in place before doors open.
2. Security keeps private and VIP areas in view, in person or by camera, at all times.
3. If a security issue comes up when the one security worker has other duties, those duties stop and security responds.
4. Security walks any dancer to their car or ride on request, at any hour.
5. Outside lighting covers the entrance, the parking lot and the path to cars. Cameras cover the entrance, the floor, the hallways to private areas and the lot.

### 2. Panic buttons and dressing rooms

**Policy.** Every room where a dancer may be alone with a customer, every restroom and every dressing room has a panic button that reaches security. Dressing rooms lock with a keypad, and only dancers and the house mom have the code.

**Procedure.**

1. Every new dancer is shown where each panic button is and how it works on their first shift.
2. The manager on duty tests every panic button at opening, or at least once a month, and logs the result.
3. Security responds to every alarm immediately. A false alarm is never held against a dancer.
4. Change the dressing-room code whenever someone who knew it leaves.

### 3. Customer conduct, warnings, ejection and bans

**Policy.** Customers follow the posted house rules. A dancer can end any dance or conversation at any time, for any reason, with no penalty. Customers who are violent, threaten anyone, or are credibly accused of violence or trafficking are banned.

**Procedure.**

1. Post the customer rules at the entrance and in private areas: no touching beyond what local law and house rules allow, no photos or video, no following dancers, no offers of sex for money, and prices agreed before a dance starts.
2. **First problem:** security gives a clear warning and tells the manager on duty.
3. **Second problem, or anything serious:** security removes the customer. Violence, threats, sexual assault, grabbing, offers of sex for money, refusing to stop after a dancer says no, or following a dancer outside all mean immediate removal, with no warning first.
4. **Ban:** the general manager adds the customer to the ban list with a description, the date and the reason. Washington requires a ban of at least three years when an accusation of violence is supported by evidence, and keeping those records at least five years. We use the same.
5. Share the ban list with door staff every shift, and with any other club under the same ownership.
6. A dancer does not have to file a police report for a customer to be banned. The club decides when to call police and writes that rule down.

### 4. Contact and no-touch rules

**Policy.** We follow our state and city rules on contact between dancers and customers, and the stricter of the law or our house rules applies. Local rules change by city and state, so check [Laws by state](laws-by-state.md).

**Procedure.**

1. Write the contact rules in plain language and post them where customers see them before any dance.
2. Security enforces the rules the same way for every customer, regular or not.
3. A dancer who stops a dance because of a rule violation keeps the full agreed price.
4. Managers never ask a dancer to break the contact rules to keep a customer happy.

### 5. Incident reports and records

**Policy.** Every incident is written down, the same shift. Records are kept private, kept at least five years, and never used against the person who reported.

**Procedure.**

1. An incident is anything that hurts or threatens someone or breaks house rules: assault, threats, harassment, grabbing, a customer following a dancer, a panic-button alarm, a refused ID, a trafficking concern, a theft, or an injury.
2. The manager on duty fills in the [incident report](#incident-report-form) before the shift ends, with the dancer's account in their own words if they want to give one.
3. The manager notes what was done: warning, removal, ban, police called, first aid, follow-up.
4. Camera footage of the incident is saved before it records over.
5. The general manager reviews every report within [two days] and looks for patterns: the same customer, the same room, the same time of night.
6. Keep a separate customer complaint log. Washington requires one.

### 6. ID and age checks

**Policy.** Every dancer's government photo ID is checked in person before their first shift, by the general manager. Dancers keep their own IDs at all times. No one under the legal age works here. The minimum age to dance is 18 in most states and 21 in some, and clubs that serve alcohol may have their own age limits. Check [Laws by state](laws-by-state.md#the-age-21-debate).

**Procedure.**

1. Check the ID in person, with the dancer present. Never accept an ID handed over by someone else.
2. Confirm the photo matches, the ID is not expired, and the dancer meets the age limit.
3. Make a copy only if state or local law requires one, keep it locked, and limit who can see it.
4. **Never hold a dancer's ID**, even for "safekeeping." Taking someone's ID is a trafficking warning sign.
5. If anything doesn't fit (the ID doesn't seem to match, the dancer seems younger than the ID, someone else answers questions for them), don't hire yet. Tell the trafficking-concern lead.
6. Check every customer's ID at the door.

### 7. Anti-trafficking screening and response

**Policy.** We do not tolerate trafficking, coercion, or sex for money. Every staff member knows the signs and what to do. No one is punished for raising a concern in good faith.

**Signs to watch for**, at hiring and on every shift:

- Someone else holds the dancer's ID, phone or money, or collects their pay.
- The same person drops them off, picks them up and seems to be in charge.
- Someone answers questions for them.
- They talk about a quota or a debt they have to work off.
- They seem afraid, watched, or younger than they say.

The full list is in [Stop trafficking](human-trafficking.md#signs-to-watch-for).

**Procedure.**

1. Pay every dancer directly. Never pay a "manager," agent, driver or partner on a dancer's behalf.
2. Staff who notice signs tell the trafficking-concern lead the same shift, quietly.
3. Don't confront the suspected controller, and don't tip them off.
4. If anyone is in immediate danger, call 911.
5. Otherwise, the lead talks privately with the dancer if it's safe, shares the hotline number, and contacts the National Human Trafficking Hotline (1-888-373-7888, text 233733) for guidance. If the person may be under 18, the lead calls 911 or the hotline right away: anyone under 18 in commercial sex is a trafficking victim under federal law.
6. Post the national hotline and your state hotline in every dressing room and restroom, in English and Spanish.
7. Zero tolerance for prostitution and for any staff member who steers customers to "extras." That's grounds for firing.

### 8. Fees, tips and pay

**Policy.** Dancers know exactly what they'll pay before they start, keep every tip, and never go into debt to the club.

**Procedure.**

1. **One written fee schedule**, the same for every dancer, handed over before the first shift and posted in the dressing room. Washington requires fee terms in a written contract that stays the same for at least three months.
2. **No fines.** No late fees, missed-shift fees, interest, or unpaid balance carried to the next shift. A dancer never owes the club money.
3. **Cap fees.** Washington's cap is a good benchmark: in any 8-hour period, no more than the lesser of $150 or 30% of what the dancer collected outside private areas, plus 30% of what the dancer collected in private areas. A fee can never be more than the dancer collected.
4. **Tips belong to the dancer.** Federal law bars employers from keeping any part of employees' tips, and managers and supervisors can't share in a tip pool. Post a sign in the dressing room that says tips don't have to be handed over.
5. **Tip-outs to staff are voluntary.** If you suggest tip-outs to the DJ, house mom or security, put them in writing and never punish a dancer who doesn't pay them.
6. **Receipts for everything.** Give a written or electronic receipt for every fee paid.
7. **Club currency ("dance dollars").** Post the exchange rate and any surcharge for customers and dancers, and pay dancers out on the same shift.
8. **Worker classification.** Have employment counsel review whether your dancers are employees or contractors at least every two years. The label in a contract doesn't decide it. See [Contractor or employee?](for-dancers.md#contractor-or-employee).

### 9. Staff training

**Policy.** Every employee is trained before or soon after their first shift, and again on a set schedule.

| Topic | Who | When |
|---|---|---|
| This policy, panic buttons, incident reports | Everyone, dancers included | First shift |
| Spotting and reporting human trafficking | All staff; dancers may join | Within 30 days of hire, then every year |
| Preventing sexual harassment, discrimination and assault | All staff; dancers may join | Within 30 days of hire, then every year |
| De-escalating conflict between dancers, staff and customers | Security, managers, door staff | Within 30 days of hire, then every year |
| First aid | Security and managers | Within 30 days of hire, then on the certification schedule |
| Responsible alcohol service | Bartenders and servers | Before serving, as your state requires |

Washington requires the trafficking, harassment and assault, de-escalation and first-aid training for all non-dancer staff, repeated at least every two years, and lets dancers opt in. Yearly is better. The U.S. Department of Homeland Security's Blue Campaign has online trafficking-awareness courses, including one for the general public. Keep a signed record of who completed what, and when.

### 10. Complaints, no retaliation and firing

**Policy.** Anyone can report a problem, including about a manager or owner, without being punished. That includes losing shifts, stage time, music or good rotation spots.

**Procedure.**

1. Name two people dancers can report to, so there's always one who isn't the person the complaint is about: [name] and [name].
2. Accept reports in person, by text or in writing, and anonymously.
3. Respond in writing within [five business days].
4. Give written reasons to any dancer you let go or won't rehire, within 10 business days. Washington requires this.

### 11. Privacy

**Policy.** We protect the privacy of dancers, staff and customers.

**Procedure.**

1. No photos or video of dancers or customers by anyone, staff included, except security cameras.
2. Never give out a dancer's legal name, phone number, address, schedule or car to anyone. "They're not working tonight" is the whole answer.
3. Keep dancer records, IDs and incident reports locked, and limit who can see them.

### 12. Alcohol and drugs

**Policy.** Alcohol is served responsibly. No one is ever pressured to drink. Staff never supply drugs to anyone.

**Procedure.**

1. Cut off visibly drunk customers, and have security watch them leave.
2. Dancers are never required to drink or to sell a set number of drinks.
3. Any staff member who supplies or sells drugs is fired. Someone supplying drugs to a dancer to control them is a trafficking warning sign.

## Forms and checklists

### Incident report form

- [ ] Date, time and place in the club
- [ ] Who was involved (staff and dancers by role or first name; customers by description)
- [ ] What happened, in the reporter's own words
- [ ] What the dancer wants to happen next, if they want to say
- [ ] Witnesses
- [ ] Panic button used? Security response time?
- [ ] Action taken: warning, removal, ban, police called, first aid
- [ ] Camera footage saved? Where?
- [ ] Follow-up owner and date
- [ ] Manager's name and signature

### Shift opening checklist

- [ ] Dedicated security on site
- [ ] Panic buttons tested, or monthly test is current
- [ ] Dressing room locked, code working
- [ ] Ban list given to door staff
- [ ] Outside lights and cameras working
- [ ] Hotline posters up in dressing rooms and restrooms
- [ ] Fee schedule and customer rules posted

### New dancer checklist

- [ ] ID checked in person by the general manager, and handed back
- [ ] Copy of this policy, the fee schedule and the customer rules given
- [ ] Shown every panic button
- [ ] Told who to report problems to, including the second contact
- [ ] Told security will walk them to their car on request
- [ ] Given the hotline numbers
