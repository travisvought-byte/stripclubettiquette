# Why it keeps happening

How trafficking and sexual exploitation show up at some strip clubs, hostess bars and cantinas, why the same places come back, why it's so hard to stop, and where to get help. For dancers, customers, families and anyone looking in.

Most dancers are adults working by choice, and dancing is not trafficking. This page is about the exceptions, and why they last. The places named here come from court and agency records on our [list of places to stay away from](../data/stay-away.csv). Everything else links to its source.

## The patterns

Polaris, the anti-trafficking nonprofit, [studied more than 32,000 hotline cases](https://polarisproject.org/news/press-releases/sex-and-labor-trafficking-redefined-typology-modern-slavery) and sorted U.S. trafficking into 25 types. "Bars, strip clubs and cantinas" is one of them, with [792 cases from 2007 to 2016](https://polarisproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Polaris-Typology-of-Modern-Slavery-1.pdf). About 95% of the victims were female, and both adults and minors were among them. These are the patterns that come up again and again.

- **The place looks ordinary.** Many of these businesses present as a normal bar, restaurant or club. Polaris found that in some, [traffickers have deals with the owners](https://polarisproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Polaris-Typology-of-Modern-Slavery-1.pdf) to run prostitution in exchange for a cut. In others, the owners run it themselves, and in some they may not know.
- **The hostess job.** In cantinas and hostess bars, women are [pushed to keep male customers company](https://polarisproject.org/typology) so they'll buy expensive drinks, often with sex expected too. Polaris says victims there are often women and girls from Mexico and Central America. Strip club cases mostly involve U.S. citizen women and girls.
- **The boyfriend.** A large share of strip club cases involve a woman [trafficked at a club by her partner](https://polarisproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Polaris-Typology-of-Modern-Slavery-1.pdf), someone with no tie to the club. The club may see an ordinary dancer.
- **The job that's too good to be true.** Recruiting runs on deception: [a real-looking job offer or a fake romance](https://polarisproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Polaris-Typology-of-Modern-Slavery-1.pdf). The Homeland Security Department's Blue Campaign lists [false promises of well-paying jobs and romantic relationships](https://www.dhs.gov/blue-campaign/what-human-trafficking) among the ways people are lured.
- **Money as the leash.** Polaris says the most common control in strip club cases is economic: [taking the worker's pay, and sometimes charging high fees](https://polarisproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Polaris-Typology-of-Modern-Slavery-1.pdf). In bars and cantinas, victims are often held by [debts they can never pay off](https://polarisproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Polaris-Typology-of-Modern-Slavery-1.pdf). In one federal case, women brought to Houston were [threatened along with their families](https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/2008/April/08_crt_263.html) if they left before paying smuggling debts. In a Polaris survey of survivors, most [reported financial abuse](https://polarisproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/In-Harms-Way-How-Systems-Fail-Human-Trafficking-Survivors-by-Polaris-modifed-June-2023.pdf), and they were twice as likely as other Americans to have no bank account.
- **Papers held.** An employer holding a worker's passport or ID is a [red flag Polaris names](https://polarisproject.org/human-trafficking/recognize-signs).

## How places come back

No government agency tracks how often a closed club or bar reopens at the same address, so there's no national count. What we know comes from court records and from research on other businesses.

- **New name, same rooms.** Polaris's research on illicit massage businesses, not clubs, describes [shops shut by police reopening under a new name and owner](https://www.mass.gov/doc/polaris-human-trafficking-in-illicit-massage-parlors-report/download), shell companies used to hide who's behind them, and licensing rules it calls an easily exploited patchwork of state and local laws.
- **It happens with clubs too.** In El Paso, a club agreed in a court order that it could no longer run as [Jaguars Gold Club](../data/stay-away.csv#SA-038). Six months later the county told the court that Chicas Locas had held a grand opening at the same address. In Houston, an appeals court said [a club on Richmond Avenue](../data/stay-away.csv#SA-039) kept operating without a license after a 2020 order to shut, and it has used at least three names.
- **Laws written to stop it.** Texas saw it in its cantinas. Since 2011, after a permit is cancelled for prostitution or trafficking, [no one can get a new on-premises liquor permit at that location for 12 months](https://www.lbb.texas.gov/Documents/Publications/Issue_Briefs/413_PSCJ_Human_Trafficking.pdf). A holder who lets a permit lapse during a trafficking hearing is barred for three years, and paying a fine instead of a suspension is no longer an option.
- **The property, not just the people.** Nuisance law goes after the building. In Texas, a place used for prostitution or trafficking can be [ordered closed for a year](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CP/pdf/CP.125.pdf), and an owner or operator is liable when they knowingly tolerate it and don't make reasonable efforts to stop it.
- **Liquor licenses move fastest.** In a 2022 Houston case, the state ordered a bar to [stop all alcohol sales for 90 days](https://www.tabc.texas.gov/news/news-releases/tabc-cancels-houston-bars-liquor-permit-following-human-trafficking-investigation-2022/) after finding a suspected brothel, then cancelled its license for good. The Texas regulator calls prostitution at a licensed bar one of the leading signs of worse organized crime, including trafficking.

What this means for you: look up the address, not just the name. Check the [stay-away list](../data/stay-away.csv) and the [club directory](../data/clubs.csv) before you go or before you audition.

## Warning signs

No single sign proves trafficking, [the Blue Campaign says](https://dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/240711_bc_what_is_human_trafficking_trifold_brochure_english.pdf). Victims can be any age, race, gender or nationality, from any background. And Polaris warns there's often [nothing you can see from across the room](https://polarisproject.org/human-trafficking/recognize-signs).

**If you're looking for work**, the National Human Trafficking Hotline lists these signs:

- A job offer that [seems too good to be true](https://humantraffickinghotline.org/en/human-trafficking/recognizing-signs)
- Owing money to an employer or recruiter, or not being paid what you were promised
- Someone else holding your passport or ID
- Living where you work, or being driven between home and work by guards

Before you apply, check that the job is real on the business's own website, and search its name with "scam" or "complaint". That's [the Federal Trade Commission's general advice](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/05/scammers-are-hijacking-job-ads-heres-how-spot-fakes) on fake job ads. Our [dancer guide](for-dancers.md) covers pay, fees and your rights at any club.

**If you're a customer or a coworker**, the [Blue Campaign](https://dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/240711_bc_what_is_human_trafficking_trifold_brochure_english.pdf) suggests asking yourself whether someone:

- Lets another person speak for them, or seems coached on what to say
- Works very long or unusual hours
- Seems to go without food, sleep or medical care, or has almost no belongings of their own
- Seems afraid, or can't come and go freely

Don't confront anyone or try a rescue. Note what you saw and [call the hotline](#getting-help). See [what to do](human-trafficking.md#what-to-do).

## Why it's hard to stop

### Gray areas

Most of the money trouble in clubs is about pay, not trafficking. Many dancers are paid as contractors. The Labor Department says a club that [sets dancers' hours, schedules and contract terms acts as an employer](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/sol/sol20250113), in a 2025 lawsuit against a Wichita club that is still an allegation. Workers treated as contractors can [lose minimum wage and overtime](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/flsa/misclassification).

A wage fight isn't trafficking. But since taking pay and charging high fees is also the most common control in strip club trafficking cases, the line can be hard to see from outside. Fees that look normal in one club can be a leash in another.

The person selling sex may be the victim. A federal research review found victims [often don't see themselves as victims](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/240701.pdf), and that arrests made "for the victim's protection" in effect treated them as suspects.

### Complicity

Sometimes the people who should stop it are paid not to.

- **A federal agent.** The owner of Pharaoh's Gentlemen's Club near Buffalo paid a Drug Enforcement Administration agent [cash bribes from 2005 to 2019](https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdny/pr/federal-jury-convicts-pharaohs-owner-multiple-charges-including-bribery-sex), and the agent used his position to steer other officers away. The owner [got 25 years](https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdny/pr/pharaohs-owner-going-prison-25-years-bribery-sex-trafficking-conspiracy-and-witness) for sex trafficking conspiracy and other crimes, and the agent got five.
- **A liquor investigator.** The owner of [a Federal Way, Washington room salon](../data/stay-away.csv#SA-031) pleaded guilty to bribing a state liquor board investigator, along with prostitution and money-laundering counts.
- **Police officers.** A former Newark officer got 46 months for [taking cash to protect brothels](https://www.justice.gov/usao-nj/pr/former-newark-police-officer-sentenced-46-months-prison-bribery-and-assisting-preparing) from police. A former D.C. officer got 27 years for [trafficking minors, including at a Maryland strip club](https://www.justice.gov/usao-dc/pr/former-metropolitan-police-department-officer-sentenced-27-years-sex-trafficking-minors).
- **Owners.** As Polaris found, some owners [take a cut or run it themselves](https://polarisproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Polaris-Typology-of-Modern-Slavery-1.pdf).

### Distrust runs both ways

- **Survivors get arrested.** In a Polaris survey of 457 survivors, [62% had been arrested, detained or cited](https://polarisproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/In-Harms-Way-How-Systems-Fail-Human-Trafficking-Survivors-by-Polaris-modifed-June-2023.pdf), most of them while they were being trafficked. Of those, 71% were left with a criminal record that made jobs and housing harder to get.
- **Most get out on their own.** The same survivors overwhelmingly said they broke free without help from police, which Polaris reads as a sign of distrust.
- **Victims stay quiet.** Federal research found victims may not report out of [fear of the trafficker, distrust of police or fear of deportation](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/240701.pdf), and that police often lacked training for these cases.
- **Dancers don't report.** In a small Portland, Oregon survey, [91% of dancers said they had never reported violence to police](https://core.ac.uk/download/212633844.pdf), citing discrimination, fear of not being believed and stigma. It was one city and a small sample.
- **Immigration as a threat.** In massage businesses, Polaris found traffickers [reporting workers to immigration](https://www.mass.gov/doc/polaris-human-trafficking-in-illicit-massage-parlors-report/download) so they'd be deported before they could talk.

### Laws that help

- **Clearing records.** Vacatur laws let survivors erase convictions tied to their trafficking, and [45 state legislatures have passed them](https://mdpi.com/2075-4698/14/2/29) since 2010. Unlike sealing, vacatur fully erases the conviction. Some laws still leave other charges on the record, and there's [no federal way to clear one](https://polarisproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/In-Harms-Way-How-Systems-Fail-Human-Trafficking-Survivors-by-Polaris-modifed-June-2023.pdf).
- **Safe harbor.** These state laws [differ a lot](https://cdpsdocs.state.co.us/ovp/Human_Trafficking/Fifty-State-Survey-Safe-Harbor-Laws-and-Expungement-Sealing-and-Vacatur-Provisions-with-Related-Statutes-for-Trafficked-Persons.pdf): some protect victims from prostitution charges, some give a defense in court, and some send minors to services instead. Laws have changed since that 2015 survey, so ask a lawyer about your state.

## Misinformation makes it worse

- **The Super Bowl myth.** A 2019 review of 55 studies found [no evidence that the Super Bowl causes or goes with a rise in sex trafficking](https://news.utexas.edu/2019/10/30/research-debunks-myth-of-super-bowl-sex-trafficking-improves-media-narrative/). The researchers say stories like it lead to arrests and stigma and [miss what victims need](https://core.ac.uk/download/234197194.pdf).
- **Viral rumors.** In 2020, a rumor about a furniture website flooded the hotline with hundreds of reports, and [none had anything beyond what was already online](https://polarisproject.org/press-releases/polaris-statement-on-wayfair-sex-trafficking-claims/). Polaris says the wave, tied to the QAnon conspiracy, [took time from real calls](https://polarisproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Polaris-2020-Annual-Report.pdf), and that [long hotline waits can decide whether someone escapes](https://polarisproject.org/blog/2020/07/how-unproven-trafficking-stories-spread-online-and-why-stopping-them-matters/).
- **The kidnapping image.** Sex trafficking is [rarely a stranger who kidnaps someone](https://polarisproject.org/blog/2020/07/how-unproven-trafficking-stories-spread-online-and-why-stopping-them-matters/). It's often someone the victim knows, loves or trusts. People watching for vans miss the boyfriend who takes the paycheck.

Report what you saw yourself. Don't pass on a viral post as a tip.

## How it keeps going

Put the pieces together, in our words. A woman who's been arrested doesn't call the police. A business that changes its name keeps the same rooms. An officer or inspector on the take steers the case away. A public that expects strangers and vans misses the debt, the held ID and the boyfriend. And false tips use up the time that real ones need. Each piece makes the next one easier.

What breaks it is what the records show working: closing the property, not just arresting the women; liquor and license rules that follow the address; clearing survivors' records; and reports based on what someone actually saw.

## Getting help

- **The National Human Trafficking Hotline** takes calls at 1-888-373-7888, texts at 233733 and online chat, [all day, every day](https://humantraffickinghotline.org/en/about-us/hotline-faqs). Advocates speak English and Spanish, and callers can get interpreters in more than 200 languages by phone.
- **You can stay anonymous.** You don't have to give your name.
- **It isn't the police.** Since September 2025 the hotline has been run by Compass Connections, a nonprofit, with federal and private funding. It doesn't share identifying details with police or agencies without your permission, except for suspected child abuse, someone in immediate danger, or where the law requires it. [Its staff are mandated reporters](https://humantraffickinghotline.org/en/report-trafficking) for those cases.
- **Immediate danger:** call 911.
- **Federal investigators:** the Homeland Security Investigations tip line is [1-866-347-2423](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/human-trafficking/ccht/stopGeneral.pdf). HSI says immigration relief is available to foreign national victims.
- **A child:** the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children takes reports at [1-800-843-5678](https://www.missingkids.org/gethelpnow/cybertipline) and online.
- **Immigration status.** Two federal protections exist for victims: [Continued Presence and the T visa](https://www.fbi.gov/file-repository/vsd/help-for-victims-of-trafficking-brochure.pdf). Under the T visa, immigration officials, not police, decide who qualifies, and the yearly cap [has never been reached](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/guides/T-Visa-Law-Enforcement-Resource-Guide.pdf). Rules change, so talk to an immigration lawyer or legal aid group.
- **Unpaid wages, illegal fees or tip deductions:** the Labor Department's Wage and Hour Division takes calls at [1-866-487-9243](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/sol/sol20250113) in more than 200 languages.
- **A record from being trafficked:** ask a legal aid lawyer whether your state's vacatur law covers it.
